How to use the chart

It works on a phone with the book in your other hand, and on a screen in front of a class.

  • Take the tour

    Fifteen stops, from the key to the gospel, each with a short explanation. Start the tour

  • Search a word or a verse

    Type rapture, Damascus, second coming, or a reference such as Matthew 24 or Rev 13:16. The view moves to the spot and marks it. Results run left to right, in timeline order.

  • Read the verse

    Tap any reference on the chart and the King James text opens beside it, so you can see at once whether the verse says what the box says.

  • Follow one line

    Pick one of the seven timelines and the view slides to it without losing your place in history.

  • Look it up

    The index of the chart lists every event and every Scripture reference on it, each linked to its place.

  • Download the chart

    Keep a copy or print it. Download the chart (about 9,700 pixels wide; prints about four and a half feet long). Your browser puts the picture together, so give it a few seconds. For the full-size file, 20,000 pixels wide, email me and I will send it. Free to download and print.

  • Read the key first

    The Timeline Key, bottom left of the chart, explains the arrows, the green anchor boxes, and the zig-zag where time is compressed. Anything marked ref explains a verse; it is not the verse itself.

  • Teach from it

    Present mode hides the controls and fills the screen. A clicker steps through the tour. Copy a link to any view and the class opens at the same spot.

The book

God has never been silent about the future.

One verse in four in the Bible is prophecy, and all of it points to one Person. Yet most believers have never seen the whole road laid out, from the promise in the garden to the city where God dwells with men. Most books on the subject either hand them a chart without the texts or the texts without the road.

A Prelude to the End of the World is that road, in order, with a King James Bible open beside it. It follows seven timelines that converge on the return of Christ. It walks through the rapture, Daniel’s seventy weeks, the seals, trumpets, and bowls, the Antichrist, Armageddon, the kingdom, and the new creation, and it stops at every step to show the reader the verses and let him check them.

It is written for the ordinary Christian who wants to know what the Bible actually says about the end, and for the pastor or study leader who needs one reference he can trust.

Seven timelines, two endings

History does not run on one track. Heaven, Israel, the church, the empires of prophecy, the earth itself, fallen man, and Satan each have a line of their own in Scripture. They do not all end in the same place.

The chart draws the seven side by side so you can see what is happening on each at the same moment. The book follows the same seven twice: once through the age we live in, and once through the seven years of the tribulation.

Watch where the lines go. From the return of Christ, Israel’s line and the believer’s are woven together. At the end heaven’s line and the earth’s meet them, and the four are woven into one where all things are made new. The prophetic line ends there, because by then every word of it has come to pass. Fallen man’s line and Satan’s go down to the lake of fire. Choose a line to follow it on the chart, or a chapter to see its subject there.

About the project

The chart was begun in January 2019 and drawn in Microsoft Paint, one box at a time. It was made to be used with a Bible: for the glory of God in showing His plans from long ago, for the encouragement and equipping of the saints, to point the person who does not yet believe to the coming judgment and to Jesus Christ as the only way of escape, and to shed light on the schemes of the devil.

The book grew out of the chart. The chart is a servant of the text and never a substitute for it. The Bible is the map, and everything here exists to send you back to it.

The chart is older than the book, and it is being revised to match it. Where the two differ today, on a date or on how firmly something is said, the book is the author’s settled view.

The author

Brandon Wallis is a Bible teacher and disciple-maker. He leads several one-on-one Bible studies each week and is a student of Faith Bible Institute, a church-based program of biblical study. He is the founder of Strangers & Pilgrims and a painter. He wrote the book because he could not find one volume that laid the whole road out, from Genesis to the new creation, for an ordinary reader with a King James Bible open beside it.
