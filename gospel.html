The gospel

The Bookshelf

Most people think of Heaven and Hell like shelves on a bookshelf. They imagine a vertical ranking system: the best people on the top shelf, the worst people on the bottom shelf, and everyone else somewhere in between. Wherever you are on the bookshelf, whatever shelf you stand on, you assume that everyone on your shelf and above goes to heaven, and everyone below you goes to Hell. The dividing line is somewhere in the middle—and as long as you are a “good enough” person, as long as your good deeds outweigh your bad ones, you make the cut.

This is how the world thinks about eternity. And it is wrong.

God does not divide the bookshelf horizontally. He divides it vertically. The dividing line is not a shelf in the middle—it is Christ. He is the line drawn straight down the center, from top to bottom. Those on one side are those who have put their faith in Him. Those on the other side have not entered by the Door, which is Christ (Jn 10:9), and they do not belong to Him. It does not matter what shelf you are on. It does not matter how the world ranks your goodness or your badness. The criminal on the cross next to Jesus was on the lowest shelf society could put a person on—and he went to paradise that very day because he turned to Christ in faith (Lk 23:42–43). The rich young ruler was on one of the highest shelves anyone could stand on—moral, wealthy, respected—and he walked away from Jesus because he would not let go of himself (Mk 10:17–22).

If you are in Christ and He is in you, then no matter what shelf you stand on, you will go to heaven to be with Him. But no matter how high your shelf is—no matter how moral your life, how generous your giving, how impressive your religious resume—you cannot go to heaven apart from faith in Jesus alone. “For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast” —Eph 2:8–9. Some read “that” as pointing to faith alone, as if faith were the gift; but the Greek word for “that,” touto, is neuter, and neither “grace” nor “faith” is, so it points to the whole of what Paul has just said: salvation by grace through faith is the gift, all of it, and none of it is of yourselves. Salvation is not a reward for the worthy. It is a gift for the willing.

The Weight of the Offended

There is an illustration that helps explain why sin against God carries an eternal consequence—a truth Anselm of Canterbury argued nine hundred years ago:1 the seriousness of an offense is measured by the worth of the one offended. Imagine you pick up a stone and walk into a junkyard. You drag the stone across the side of a rusted, abandoned car. No one cares. The car was already worthless. There is no fine, no consequence, no damage worth measuring. Now take that same stone to a used car lot and drag it across the paint of a car for sale. You will be confronted. You will receive a fine. You will be required to pay for the damage. The scratch is the same. The stone is the same. The person who did it is the same. But the consequence is greater—because the value of what was damaged is greater.

Now take that same stone to a brand-new car dealership and drag it across the side of a vehicle fresh off the factory floor. The trouble increases significantly. The cost of repair is higher. The legal consequences are more serious. The offense has not changed. The stone has not changed. The person scratching the car has not changed. But the value of what was offended has gone up, and with it, the severity of the punishment. Now take that same stone to the most expensive car on the planet—a one-of-a-kind, top-of-the-line sports car worth millions—and drag it across the finish. The consequences are now enormous. You may face criminal charges. You may owe more than you could ever repay in a lifetime. Same stone. Same scratch. Same person. But the value of what was damaged determines the weight of the punishment.

This is the principle behind the severity of God’s judgment. The offense of sin does not change. A lie is a lie. A lustful thought is a lustful thought. A moment of pride is a moment of pride. And the one committing the offense does not change—we are finite, limited human beings. What changes everything is the One who is offended. God is not a junkyard car. He is not a used vehicle. He is not even the most expensive sports car on the planet. He is the infinite, eternal, holy Creator of the universe—the One whose value and worth are beyond all measurement. When a finite creature sins against an infinitely holy God, the offense carries infinite weight—not because the sin itself is infinite, but because the One sinned against is.

This is why the punishment for sin is eternal. It is not because God is cruel. It is not because the punishment does not fit the crime. It is because the value of the One offended demands a consequence that matches His worth. An infinite God, offended by the breaking of His commandments and the violation of His holy standard, requires an infinite payment. And that is what Jesus provided on the cross—an infinite sacrifice, by an infinite Person, to pay an infinite debt that no finite human being could ever pay on their own.

No One Is Beyond Reach

If you have read the lists of sins that exclude people from the kingdom of God (Rev 21:8; 1 Cor 6:9–10; Gal 5:19–21; Eph 5:5; Rev 22:15), you may have seen yourself in those lists. You may be thinking that the gospel is for other people, cleaner people, people whose failures are not as deep as yours. But the same apostle who wrote those lists also wrote this: “And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified [set apart], but ye are justified [declared righteous] in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God” —1 Cor 6:11. Were. Past tense. The fornicators were washed. The idolaters were sanctified. The thieves, the drunkards, the swindlers—they were justified. Every sin on every list was represented in the church at Corinth, and every one of those people was rescued by the same gospel being offered to you right now. No one who comes to Christ is turned away.

Jesus said it Himself: “All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out” —Jn 6:37. Never. Regardless of what you have done, where you have been, or how far you have fallen. Scripture names one exception, and it belongs to the tribulation, not to today: those who worship the beast and receive his mark have made a final choice (see Rev 14:9–11). Until that day, no one is beyond reach.

What Happens When You Believe

When you place your faith in Jesus Christ, something happens that goes far beyond a change of mind or a decision to live differently. A transaction takes place in the spiritual realm that cannot be undone. Paul described it: “Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature [creation]: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new” —2 Cor 5:17. You are not improved. You are not reformed. You are made new.

At that moment, the Holy Spirit takes up permanent residence inside you: “What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost [Holy Spirit] which is in you, which ye have of God…” —1 Cor 6:19. “Ghost” in KJV English simply meant spirit. You are sealed by the Spirit as a guarantee of your inheritance: “In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise, Which is the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession…” —Eph 1:13–14.

The word “earnest” means a guarantee or deposit—the Holy Spirit is God’s down payment, securing our inheritance until the day of redemption. And you are transferred out of the domain of darkness and into the kingdom of God’s beloved Son: “Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son: In whom we have redemption through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins” —Col 1:13–14. The word “translated” here does not mean converting language—it means relocated from one kingdom to another. This is not something you feel happening. It is something God does, and it is finished the moment you believe.

A Gift That Cannot Be Lost

One of the most important truths a new believer needs to understand is that salvation, once received, cannot be lost. It is not held in place by your performance, your faithfulness, or your ability to avoid sin. It is held in place by God. Jesus said it Himself: “And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father’s hand” —Jn 10:28–29. You are in the Son’s hand, and the Son’s hand is in the Father’s hand. No one—not Satan, not the world, not even you yourself—can pry those hands open.

Paul drove this home with one of the most sweeping declarations in all of Scripture: “For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities [ruling authorities], nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord” —Rom 8:38–39. Nothing in all creation. That includes your worst day, your deepest failure, and your most persistent sin. The gift of eternal life is eternal—not probationary, not conditional, not contingent on your ability to hold on. God holds on to you. This principle was established long before the cross.

When God made His covenant with Abraham in Genesis 15, He instructed Abraham to cut animals in half and lay them out for a covenant ceremony. In the ancient world, both parties would walk between the pieces, binding themselves to the terms. But God put Abraham into a deep sleep (Gen 15:12). Abraham did not walk. Abraham could not walk. He was unconscious. And then God alone—represented by a smoking furnace and a burning lamp—passed between the pieces (Gen 15:17). God walked both sides. He took upon Himself the obligations of both parties. The covenant did not depend on Abraham’s faithfulness—it depended on God’s. Salvation works the same way.

God does not force anyone to come to Him—the decision to trust Christ is yours, freely made. But once you have believed, the keeping of that salvation is His work, not yours. He made the promise. He paid the price. And He is the One who holds you secure. Just as Abraham contributed nothing to the covenant except his faith, the believer contributes nothing to salvation except believing. Everything else—the sacrifice, the commitment, the guarantee—God did Himself.

Does that mean a believer may live as he pleases? Paul heard the question in his own day and answered it: “…Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid…” —Rom 6:1–2. A child of God who goes on in sin does not lose his place in the family. He meets his Father. “For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth [disciplines], and scourgeth [whips] every son whom he receiveth” —Heb 12:6. That discipline can be severe. Some believers at Corinth were weak, some were sick, and some had died under it (1 Cor 11:30). Yet Paul gives the reason in the same breath: “But when we are judged, we are chastened of the Lord, that we should not be condemned with the world” —1 Cor 11:32. The son is disciplined because he will never be condemned. And what he wasted will be counted at the judgment seat of Christ, where a believer can suffer loss and still be saved. Grace does not make sin safe. It makes the sinner a son, and a good Father does not leave His sons alone.

How can you know it took? Not by how you feel. Some people weep when they believe and some feel nothing at all, and neither is the evidence. The evidence is the promise. John wrote his letter for exactly this reason: “These things have I written unto you that believe on the name of the Son of God; that ye may know that ye have eternal life…” —1 Jn 5:13. Not hope. Know. Jesus said, “…He that believeth on me hath everlasting life” —Jn 6:47—has, present tense, the moment he believes. If you have trusted Christ, you have what He said you have, on the day you feel it and on the day you do not, because your assurance rests on His Word and not on your pulse.

The Door Is Still Open

The world is moving toward a fixed and certain end. The signs are converging and quickening. The fig tree is blossoming. The Day is drawing near. But right now—in this moment, on this page—the door is still open. Jesus said, “I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved…” —Jn 10:9. He also said, “Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him, and will sup [dine] with him, and he with me” —Rev 3:20. Christ offers intimate fellowship, sitting down at the table with anyone who opens the door.

The lake of fire was not prepared for you. It was prepared for the devil and his angels (Matt 25:41). The new heavens and the new earth—the place where righteousness dwells, where God wipes away all tears, where believers see His face—that was prepared for you. Jesus said, “In my Father’s house are many mansions [dwelling places]: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you.” —Jn 14:2. He has prepared a place. He has paid the price. He is offering it freely.

The same Jesus who is coming back to judge the nations is the One who died to save anyone who will trust Him. He is not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance (2 Pet 3:9). The invitation has stood since the cross, and it stands right now: “Come.”

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life. For God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world; but that the world through him might be saved” —Jn 3:16–17.

The Three Steps

There are two endings, and every reader will meet one of them. Jesus Christ is the only way of escape, and the Bible puts it simply.

  1. A

    Acknowledge that you have sinned

    Not some people. All of us.

    “For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;” Rom 3:23
    “For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.” Rom 6:23
  2. B

    Believe that Jesus is Lord and that God raised Him from the dead

    God did not leave us without a rescue. Christ took the sinner’s place.

    “But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.” Rom 5:8
    “For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.” 2 Cor 5:21
  3. C

    Call on the name of the Lord

    Words do not save; faith does. A prayer is how faith speaks, not a formula God is waiting for.

    “That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.” Rom 10:9
    “For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.” Rom 10:13

Salvation is not a reward for the worthy. It is a gift for the willing.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.” Eph 2:8–9

What to Do Now

If you have believed, four things come next, and the first is the one most people ask about. Be baptized. Baptism does not save—the thief went from the cross to paradise without it, and Paul was careful to say that Christ sent him “…not to baptize, but to preach the gospel…” —1 Cor 1:17. But it is the first thing Jesus commanded His people to do with a new believer (Matt 28:19), and on the day the church began, those who believed were baptized the same day (Acts 2:41). It is the public announcement that you belong to Him, and a believer who refuses it is refusing his first act of obedience. Find a church that will do it in water, as Jesus was, and do it soon.

Then, read the Word—start with the Gospel of John, then Romans, and keep going, because faith comes by hearing it (Rom 10:17). Pray; you have a Father now, and He is listening (Matt 6:9). Find a church where the Bible is taught for what it says and the gospel is preached plainly, and go, because no one was meant to walk this alone (Heb 10:25). And tell someone—this week, by name—what has happened to you. The first thing Andrew did after he found the Messiah was find his brother (Jn 1:41). Start there.
